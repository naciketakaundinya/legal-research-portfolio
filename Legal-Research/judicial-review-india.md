# Judicial Review in India

## Introduction

Judicial Review is the power of the constitutional courts to examine the validity of legislative and executive actions and to invalidate them when they violate the Constitution.

In India, Judicial Review plays an important role in maintaining constitutional supremacy, protecting Fundamental Rights and ensuring that public authorities act within constitutional limits.

## Constitutional Framework

The power of Judicial Review in India is primarily connected with:

- Article 13 – Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of inconsistency.
- Article 32 – Provides the right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
- Article 226 – Gives High Courts the power to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for other purposes.
- Article 136 – Provides the Supreme Court with discretionary appellate jurisdiction.
- Articles 245 and 246 – Define legislative powers and their constitutional limitations.

## Key Features

1. Protection of Fundamental Rights
2. Preservation of Constitutional Supremacy
3. Review of Legislative and Executive Actions
4. Prevention of Abuse of Constitutional Powers
5. Maintenance of the Basic Structure of the Constitution

## Landmark Cases

### 1. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

The Supreme Court established the Basic Structure Doctrine. Parliament has the power to amend the Constitution, but it cannot alter its basic structure.

### 2. Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)

The Supreme Court recognised that certain fundamental constitutional principles cannot be destroyed through constitutional amendment.

### 3. Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India (1980)

The Supreme Court reaffirmed the importance of limited amending power and held that Judicial Review is an important part of the constitutional structure.

### 4. L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)

The Supreme Court held that the power of Judicial Review exercised by the Supreme Court under Article 32 and High Courts under Article 226 forms an essential part of the Constitution's basic structure.

## Importance of Judicial Review

Judicial Review ensures that:

- Laws comply with the Constitution.
- Executive authorities remain within their legal powers.
- Fundamental Rights receive judicial protection.
- Constitutional limitations are respected.
- The rule of law is maintained.

## Conclusion

Judicial Review is one of the most important mechanisms for maintaining constitutional governance in India. It ensures that legislative and executive powers are exercised within constitutional boundaries and provides an effective safeguard for Fundamental Rights and the Basic Structure of the Constitution.
